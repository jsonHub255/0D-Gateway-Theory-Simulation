\begin{abstract}
We evaluate quantum tunneling transmission probabilities across a 3D potential barrier with a localized 4D spatial interaction constrained to the origin $(0,0,0)$. Using Bethe-Peierls boundary conditions, we regularize zero-range interactions to ensure grid-scale independence and derive the metric projection of the 4D coordinate onto the 3D Euclidean hypersurface.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The hypothesis posits a spatial intersection of a higher-dimensional coordinate $w$ at the zero-dimensional origin point $(0,0,0)$ in 3D Euclidean space.

\section{Renormalization Group (RG) Flow \& Grid Invariance}
To ensure the physical validity of the origin coupling, the bare coupling constant $g(\sigma)$ must be renormalized as the spatial grid resolution parameter $\sigma \to 0$. We express the regularized potential $V_{\text{reg}}(\mathbf{r})$ using a normalized Gaussian envelope:

\begin{equation}
V_{\text{reg}}(\mathbf{r}) = -g(\sigma) \left(2\pi \sigma^2\right)^{-3/2} \exp\left(-\frac{r^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
\end{equation}

Matching $V_{\text{reg}}(\mathbf{r})$ to the low-energy s-wave scattering amplitude determined by the Bethe-Peierls boundary condition $\psi(r) \sim C(1/r - 1/a_s)$ yields the explicit RG equation for the bare coupling:

\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{g(\sigma)} = \frac{m}{4\pi \hbar^2 a_s} + \frac{m}{4\pi \hbar^2 \sigma \sqrt{2\pi}}
\end{equation}

Solving for $g(\sigma)$ gives the continuous scaling relation:

\begin{equation}
g(\sigma) = \frac{4\pi \hbar^2}{m} \left( \frac{1}{a_s} - \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \right)^{-1}
\end{equation}

As $\sigma \to 0$, $g(\sigma)$ naturally flows to absorb the $1/\sigma$ divergence, holding the physical s-wave scattering length $a_s$ constant and rendering observable transmission probabilities invariant under grid refinement.

\section{4D-to-3D Metric Tensor Projection}
To provide a geometric origin for the localized boundary term, we evaluate the dimensional reduction of a 4D metric tensor $g_{MN}$ onto a 3D Euclidean hypersurface $\Sigma_3$ at $w=0$. 

We define the line element of the 4D bulk space using an origin-localized warp factor $\Phi(r)$:

\begin{equation}
ds^2 = g_{MN} dX^M dX^N = g_{\mu\nu}(x) dx^\mu dx^\nu + e^{2\Phi(r)} dw^2
\end{equation}

where $X^M = (x^\mu, w)$ with $\mu \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, $r = \sqrt{\sum (x^\mu)^2}$, and $w$ represents the fourth spatial dimension.

\subsection{Hypersurface Projection \& Extrinsic Curvature}
The induced metric $h_{\mu\nu}$ on the 3D hypersurface $w = 0$ is obtained via the projection tensor $P^{\,M}_{\mu} = \frac{\partial X^M}{\partial x^\mu}$:

\begin{equation}
h_{\mu\nu} = g_{MN} P^{\,M}_{\mu} P^{\,N}_{\nu} = g_{\mu\nu}
\end{equation}

The unit normal vector to the $w=0$ hypersurface is given by $n^M = (0, 0, 0, e^{-\Phi(r)})$. The extrinsic curvature tensor $K_{\mu\nu}$ measuring the geometric bending of the 3D space into the 4D bulk takes the form:

\begin{equation}
K_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} \mathcal{L}_n h_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} e^{-\Phi(r)} \frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial w}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Zero-Point Singular Limit}
Assuming a Gaussian warp profile $\Phi(r) = -\frac{r^2}{2\sigma^2}$, the trace of the extrinsic curvature $K = h^{\mu\nu} K_{\mu\nu}$ concentrates strictly at the origin $r \to 0$:

\begin{equation}
\lim_{\sigma \to 0} K(r) \propto \delta^3(\mathbf{r})
\end{equation}

This curvature singularity produces an effective delta-like potential well in the 3D effective Hamiltonian, which matches the regularized s-wave scattering length $a_s$ derived under the Bethe-Peierls boundary conditions:

\begin{equation}
\psi(r) \sim C \left( \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{a_s} \right) \quad \text{as } r \to 0
\end{equation}
